AppendixThe physics

The galaxy is rendered live from one gravitational potential and shown the way Hubble would show it: near edge-on at 84°, with an asinh display stretch, a four-vane diffraction pattern on the foreground stars, and a dust brim that scatters as well as absorbs. The rotation curve below and the motion on screen are computed from the same numbers, so the spin you see is the spin the halo requires. Colours and brightness are artistic. The structure is not.

It spins the right way roundΩ(R) = vc/R from the potential, so the inner disk laps the outer disk. The m = 2 arms trail, and they turn rigidly at the pattern speed while stars stream through them.
Stars are born downstream of the dustInside corotation, gas overtakes the arm, shocks at the dust lane and forms OB stars and H II regions just beyond it. Past corotation the order reverses.
The winding problem is avoidedDust clumps are advected by differential rotation in two staggered flow-map layers, so they shear into arcs and are replaced before they wind up.
Dark matter ignores the dust laneStars are reddened by the ring (RV ≈ 3.1 ratios per channel). Halo particles are drawn without extinction, because they don't interact with light.
The brim is brown, not blackThe near side of the ring sits inside the spheroid, so starlight in front of it fills the lane in, light behind it is reddened on the way through, and the grains scatter bluer light back out. Dust is both absorber and reflector.
The spikes are the telescope, not the skyFour struts hold Hubble's secondary mirror; their diffraction pattern is a cross on every bright point source. The pass is applied only to unresolved sources, with red light spreading further along the spike than blue.
The web keeps movingPast z = 0 the Zel’dovich flow continues at an exaggerated rate: sheets stick where they first cross (adhesion) and oscillate through the pancake, while filaments drain into the node.
