The part you can't see does most of the work.

About 85% of the Sombrero's mass is dark. It never touches the light, yet it sets every orbit you see. Omnia works the same way: one coherent layer underneath your services, data and teams, keeping the whole system in shape while the visible parts do their jobs.

AppendixThe physics in the logo

An art piece, built on real models.

The mark is rendered live from one gravitational potential. The rotation curve below and the motion on screen are computed from the same numbers, so the spin you see is the spin the halo requires. Colours and brightness are artistic. The structure is not.

ComponentModelValues usedReference
It spins the right way roundΩ(R) = vc/R from the potential, so the inner disk laps the outer disk. The m = 2 arms trail, and they turn rigidly at the pattern speed while stars stream through them.
Stars are born downstream of the dustInside corotation, gas overtakes the arm, shocks at the dust lane and forms OB stars and H II regions just beyond it. Past corotation the order reverses.
The winding problem is avoidedDust clumps are advected by differential rotation in two staggered flow-map layers, so they shear into arcs and are replaced before they wind up.
Dark matter ignores the dust laneStars are reddened by the ring (RV ≈ 3.1 ratios per channel). Halo particles are drawn without extinction, because they don't interact with light.
